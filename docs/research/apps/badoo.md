## 1. Snapshot

- **Positioning.** Older (2006) mass-market social-dating app, strong outside the US: People Nearby, Encounters (swipe), Video Chat, Live video. Part of Bumble Inc. (Badoo App and Other segment).
- **Markets.** Wikipedia says 190 countries and 47 languages [unverified, encyclopedic summary] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badoo). Sagaci's African survey says Badoo is close behind Tinder in Africa and stronger in francophone countries such as Cameroon (https://sagaciresearch.com/dating-apps-in-africa-usage-trends-and-popular-apps/). Business of Apps claims 55 million users in 2022 and $209M revenue [secondary] (https://www.businessofapps.com/?p=89085). A 410 million registered-users claim exists for 2019 [low-quality page, do not use].
- **Business model.** Freemium: premium subscriptions plus in-app purchases (credits, "Super Powers") (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badoo).
- **Reported scale (official).** Badoo App and Other paying users: 1.4M (2020), 1.2M (2022, after the March 2022 removal of apps from stores in Russia and Belarus), about 1.2M (2023, ARPPU $12.70), average 1,342.0 thousand in 2024 (Bumble 10-Ks: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1830043/000095017024022104/bmbl-20231231.htm; https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250218678439/en/). Q2 2026: Badoo App and Other revenue $38.8M, down 17.1% (https://ir.bumble.com/news/news-details/2026/Bumble-Inc--Announces-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx). Note the huge gap between claimed users and about 1.2M payers.
- **History worth knowing.** June 2018: announced it would test dropping swipe-based Encounters for live video, citing its own survey of 5,000 users (68% disliked swiping, 74% wanted more real-life experiences) (https://www.globaldatinginsights.com/news/badoo-is-removing-swiping-from-its-app/; https://techcrunch.com/?p=1652392). Wikipedia says the livestream feature was removed 16 April 2020. The App Store listing today still shows Encounters (https://apps.apple.com/US/app/id351331194).

- Photo verification (2016): user receives a request to photograph themselves performing a specific, unusual gesture; human moderators (about 5,000 moderators cited) compare to profile photos; verified users get a badge (https://www.globaldatinginsights.com/news/badoo-add-photo-verification-to-dating-platform/). - What verification unlocks: visibility to users filtering for verified profiles (TechCrunch 2016).

## 8. Why they do it, and lessons

**Reasoning (evidenced).**
- Verification at sign-up with a visibility penalty rather than a block: nudges verification without locking people out (TechCrunch 2016). This is the closest primary-source example of "verification with a soft consequence".
- Swipe fatigue: Badoo's own 2018 survey figures (68% disliked swiping) explain the live-video experiment. It was reversed by 2020, a signal that novelty alone did not stick [inference].
- Large registered base but only about 1.2 to 1.3M payers: monetisation is the hard part for mass social apps [inference from filings].

**Lessons for Marriage Connect.**
1. **HIGH.** Make verification a visible choice with a stated consequence ("unverified profiles are shown less to people who want verified only") rather than a gate in front of the first screen. This is the pattern Badoo used in 2016.
2. **HIGH.** Let people filter for verified-only: it turns verification into a benefit for the verifier's audience.
3. **MED.** Human moderation backs verification at launch; plan reviewer capacity in Uganda for ID+selfie failures.
4. **MED.** Multiple discovery modes (nearby vs encounters) help sparse pools; relevant for a small city-limited pool.
5. **MED.** Sell nothing that hides who liked you until you pay unless disclosed; Badoo does it under Premium.
6. **LOW.** Live video: costly in bandwidth, poor fit with data costs.
7. **LOW.** Credits for visibility boost: pay-for-visibility conflicts with fairness in a marriage context.
8. **LOW.** Treat Badoo's user-count claims with suspicion; they are not what filings show.

## 9. Anti-patterns to avoid

- Claiming huge user numbers that your own filings contradict (note for competitor reading).
- Selling visibility (credits) in a product whose promise is mutual seriousness.
- A verification flow whose pass/fail rules are inconsistent across public sources (we do not know Badoo's actual current rule; document ours clearly).
- Letting "Super Powers"-style add-ons sit beside subscriptions with unclear differences.
