## 1. Snapshot

- **Positioning.** The longest-running "science-based, serious relationship / marriage" matcher. Compatibility Matching System patented May 2004; questionnaire named "29 Dimensions of Compatibility", grouped into six sections; original profile had 436 items; matching reportedly required at least 25 of 29 dimensions (https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/eharmonycom-inc ; https://www.datingnews.com/apps-and-sites/facts-about-eharmonys-algorithm/). The company says the model rests on research with more than 5,000 married people; a 2005 New Jersey settlement required disclosing that it was developed from married heterosexual couples (same sources). The company's own claims are not independently validated.
- **Ownership.** ParshipMeet Group, a joint venture of ProSiebenSat.1 Media and General Atlantic; bought in 2018 via NuCom (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EHarmony ; https://www.globaldatinginsights.com/news/eharmony-acquired-by-parship-umbrella/).
- **Markets.** US, Canada, Australia, UK and others (eharmony.com, .co.uk, .com.au, .ca domains exist). A current country list: not found. Uganda: not found.
- **Scale (dated).** 2017 report: about 750,000 paid subscribers and 10 million active users; 2018 deal: expected 2.8 million new users that year; revenue about $250M dates to 2009 (Wikipedia/Reuters summaries). Current figures: not found.
- **Model.** Freemium on paper, paid in practice: multi-month prepaid subscriptions (section 5).

## 2. Sign-up / onboarding flow, step by step

**Source quality.** No Pageflows or Mobbin teardown retrieved. The steps combine eHarmony's own FAQ/free-dating pages (as summarised in search) and review sites (https://www.eharmony.com/free-dating/ ; https://www.eharmony.com/tour/eharmony-cost/ ; https://www.eharmony.com/tour/how-does-eharmony-work/ ; https://www.datingnews.com/apps-and-sites/how-does-eharmony-work/ ; https://www.vice.com/en/via/eharmony-review/).

**Current flow (reconstructed):**
1. Create an account with email (or username and password), sometimes after entering gender, who you seek and basics (several reviews).
2. **Compatibility Quiz: about 80 multiple-choice questions.** eHarmony says most people finish in about 20 minutes; Vice says 30-45 minutes; others say 15-45 (https://www.eharmony.ca/tour/what-is-the-compatibility-quiz/ ; Vice). Reviews say you cannot see matches or communicate until it is done (Vice, AARP: https://www.aarp.org/family-relationships/eharmony-review/).
3. **Add at least one photo**, after which the profile becomes visible to others (reviewer summary). Up to 12 photos allowed; 3-7 advised.
4. **Daily matches** are generated; "a handful of suggestions" per day.
5. Optional: icebreakers and communication tools (not detailed in what I saw).

**Permissions timing, skippable fields, failure handling:** not found.

**History of the questionnaire (important evidence).**
- Dec 2016: new CEO Grant Langston made the roughly 150-question survey **optional**; a basic profile took about ten minutes, and the company added real-time chat. His reason: "Science tells us that our product works... but the way we deliver it feels a bit dusty." TechCrunch asked whether it was a short-term sign-up play; no conversion data was reported (https://money.cnn.com/2016/12/16/technology/eharmony-removed-questionnaire/index.html ; https://techcrunch.com/2016/12/15/eharmony-makes-its-questionnaire-optional-to-get-hip-with-the-times ; https://www.globaldatinginsights.com/news/new-eharmony-ceo-modernises-product-ditching-questionnaire-adding-real-time-chat/).
- Later: a new 80-question quiz relaunched; GDI reported record membership growth with UK subscriptions up 50% year on year over the holiday period and Gen Z/Millennial registrations up 53%, credited to the new test (probably early 2020; the article is undated in the summary) (https://www.globaldatinginsights.com/news/new-compatibility-testing-contributes-to-record-growth-for-eharmony/). A 2026 review says the quiz is "now mandatory" again (https://www.datingadvice.com/review/eharmony). **Growth was confounded by seasonality and a platform relaunch, so this is not evidence the quiz caused growth.**

**Time-to-first-value.** The first matches appear only after a ~20-45 minute quiz plus a photo: the longest of the five apps (confidence: medium, mixed sources).

**What the user sees before committing.** Marketing and the quiz itself. Registration and the quiz are free; "free accounts never expire" (https://www.eharmony.com.au/tour/faq/what-is-free-and-what-do-i-have-to-pay-for/ , summary).

**Exact current free vs paid model (re-check live).**
- Official US pages (summarised): the free **Basic** membership lets you explore members' profiles, see unlimited matches, send/receive **Likes** and send an **initial message** free; **Premium** adds seeing photos and unlimited messaging (https://www.eharmony.com/free-dating/ ; https://www.eharmony.com/tour/faq/what-are-your-membership-options/). The UK page also says everyone can send a first message.
- Older/third-party and Australian pages conflict: photos blurred for free users; only paid users can send messages; free users get limited messages or smiles. The change date is not stated anywhere I saw.
- **Conclusion:** eHarmony has historically been "free to register and see matches, paid to communicate"; current US pages loosen this to free Likes and a first message, with photos and unlimited chat paid. Treat as **[unverified until checked in the app]**.

## 3. First-session experience and core loop

- Introductions are **daily matches** pre-ranked by the compatibility score; no swipe feed (Dating News; AARP). Compatibility results include a personality report visible to potential matches (AARP).
- Pacing: a handful a day; pace is slow by design to avoid "decision fatigue" (review claim).
- Guided communication: conversation starters and prompts exist; the exact staged process: not found.
- Empty states and notifications: not found.

## 4. Profile building

- **Minimum:** quiz complete + one photo (above). The quiz both builds the profile (personality summary) and drives ranking.
- **Photos:** up to 12; advice 3-7.
- **Verification:** no selfie-verification badge found. One undated blog says a paid third-party identity check (RelyID) exists for about $5.95/year **[unverified]**. Truth in Advertising lists allegations that eHarmony asked for a selfie in account verification without adequately disclosing why or how long it stores it; outcome not given (https://truthinadvertising.org/brands/eharmony/).

## 5. Monetisation

- **Plans.** Prepaid multi-month Premium plans only (commonly 6, 12 and 24 months: Light, Plus, Extra), no month-to-month option; all features the same, only length changes the per-month rate; billed upfront; auto-renew, often at a higher rate than the introductory price (https://www.vidaselect.com/eharmony-cost ; https://www.datingnews.com/movers-and-shakers/eharmony-cost/ ; https://www.datingadvice.com/online-dating/why-is-eharmony-so-expensive).
- **Prices (US, third-party, widely inconsistent).** Roughly $24-73/month equivalent depending on term; the App Store lists Premium Light at $179.99 to $349.99 upfront per one source. Seasonal discounts 10-50%. **No Uganda pricing found.**
- **Paywall triggers.** Seeing photos; unlimited messaging; replying beyond the free first message (per official pages summarised). Exact in-app UI moment: not found.
- **Guarantee.** Reportedly free extension if unhappy after three months, conditional on a 3-month+ paid plan (review site).
- **Regulators and consumer criticism.**
  - California: district attorneys of four counties and Santa Monica sued over automatic renewal without clear disclosure of renewal, amount or how to cancel; 2018 judgment: up to $1 million in customer restitution for people enrolled 10 March 2012 to 13 December 2016, plus a $1.28 million civil penalty, easy cancellation required, effective within one business day (https://kleinmoynihan.com/automatic-renewal-lawsuit-settlement/ ; https://www.subscriptioninsider.com/regulation-compliance/eharmony-to-pay-more-than-2-million-to-settle-california-claims).
  - Australia: the ACCC has an active case alleging failure to display pricing and cancellation options accurately, with hundreds of complaints (https://www.sydneycriminallawyers.com.au/?p=64962 ; Vice). Outcome not found.

## 6. Safety and trust features

Limited public evidence: no verified badge found; the main trust mechanism is the cost and effort barrier (quiz plus paid plan), which reviewers describe as a filter for serious users (Vice). Reporting/blocking details: not found.

## 7. Accessibility and ease of use

No accessibility statement found. Ease: a 80-question quiz on a phone is the main load; reviewers note it filters out the casual (Vice). Cancellation difficulty is the subject of the legal actions above.

## 8. Why they do what they do, and transferable lessons

**Evidenced reasoning.**
- The founding premise: compatibility science from research on married couples; the long quiz is both the product and a commitment filter (Vice's wording: it "filters for people serious enough to sit through" the quiz).
- In 2016 the CEO judged the delivery "dusty" and cut the survey to attract sign-ups; they later reinstated a shorter 80-question quiz and reported growth (GDI), suggesting that the quiz was kept as a differentiator rather than dropped.
- Academic critique: Finkel et al. (2012) argue similarity-based matching algorithms are unlikely to work as advertised; there is no independent peer-reviewed validation of the 29 dimensions in what I found (https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/journals/pspi/online-dating.html).

**Lessons.**
1. **HIGH.** The most relevant precedent: a marriage-seeking brand tried optional vs mandatory questionnaires. It shows a mandatory compatibility quiz can work commercially for a serious-intent audience, but only with free registration and transparency about duration.
2. **HIGH.** State the quiz length ("about 20 minutes") up front and let users save progress; eHarmony's own estimate vs reviewers' 30-45 min shows expectations drift.
3. **HIGH.** Registration and the quiz stay free even when messaging is paid; the user invests before any payment.
4. **HIGH.** Reject long fixed-term auto-renewing contracts. California and ACCC actions show regulatory risk; ours (web prepaid, no auto-renew) is the safer pattern.
5. **MED.** A personality summary visible to matches turns the quiz output into profile content, so the effort shows up on the profile.
6. **MED.** A small number of daily matches suits a marriage frame, with an honest limit.
7. **MED.** Free first message / likes lowers the "paid to say hello" barrier compared with the old model.
8. **LOW.** A 3-month guarantee depends on conditions that regulators have attacked on the sister brand Match; avoid guarantees.
9. **LOW.** Price anchors ($20-70/month) do not transfer.

## 9. Anti-patterns to avoid

- Quiz with no time disclosure and no progress save, as the only gate to any value.
- Opaque, upfront-billed multi-year plans that auto-renew at higher prices.
- Blurred photos as the primary conversion lever (reported for free users in older/third-party descriptions).
- Collecting selfies without stating purpose and retention (Truth in Advertising allegation).
- Cancellation that is hard to find or slow.
