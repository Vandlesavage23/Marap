## 0. Our current model (from the repo)

Source: `CLAUDE.md` rules 1, 9; `docs/BUILD_BIBLE.md` section 10.3, 10.7.

- Flow: phone OTP, consents, mandatory ID + selfie verification (Smile ID), profile go-live (>= 2 approved photos, approved description, core questionnaire), explicit 24 h trial, then membership.
- Capability table (10.7): not verified = no browse, no send; trial/member = browse + send + message; none/expired = no browse, no send, but can receive/accept interest and read chats.
- Consequence the owner observed: a new user sees no discovery content until they have passed ID verification AND started a trial. Value is delivered last, cost (friction, data, commitment) first.
- Rule 9 (no dark patterns) is a binding constraint on any option below: no fake countdowns, no hidden renewal, no streaks.

## 1. Evidence

### 1.1 When to show the paywall: value first

- Apple's own review rules require that, before asking someone to subscribe, you "clearly describe what the user will get for the price" and that you must not "trick users into purchasing a subscription under false pretenses or engage in bait-and-switch". Apple also says "If your app doesn't include significant account-based features, let people use it without a login" and "Apps may not require users to enter personal information to function, except when directly relevant to the core functionality of the app or required by law." App Store Review Guidelines 3.1.2(a), 3.1.2(c), 5.1.1(v). https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/ (fetched directly). Dating arguably has "significant account-based features" and ID data "directly relevant" to safety, so this does not forbid our verification, but it does set the standard we will be reviewed against: do not demand more personal data than needed before the user can see what the app does. Reviewers can also be refused a demo path: guideline 2.1(a) requires an active demo account or fully-featured demo mode for account-based apps (same URL, via fetched summary). This matters for us: Apple/Google reviewers cannot pass SMS OTP + real government ID, so a review-only path is needed regardless of product decision. [action item, flag for release docs]
- RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2025 (full report is gated; 263 pages): https://revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025 (listed in search; not fetched). Secondary summaries: median Day-35 download-to-paid 12.1% for hard paywalls vs 2.2% for freemium (2025 edition); a later edition summary gives 10.7% vs 2.1% (secondary: https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/paywall-conversion-structural-decisions , https://www.rocketshiphq.com/?p=5478 , https://pricepush.app/blog/trial-to-paid-conversion-rate-benchmarks). Same Airbridge write-up reports refund rates 5.8% hard vs 3.4% freemium and year-1 LTV $49.30 vs $24.24 (secondary, single source). Caveat that matters: these are cross-category medians dominated by utility/AI/health apps with a single-user value proposition. A two-sided marketplace (dating) needs supply-side liquidity: a hard paywall that blocks browsing also blocks the content other users came for. No source I found gives hard-vs-freemium numbers for dating specifically. [unverified for dating]
- RevenueCat summary via SaaStr: freemium "does not retain better; it simply converts worse" (secondary: https://www.saastr.com/the-top-10-learnings-from-revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps/). The same sources say hard-paywall gating "only works when onboarding shows value first" (Airbridge, secondary).
- Day-0 timing: a SaaStr summary of the 2026-edition data says 50% of paid conversions happen on Day 0 (secondary: https://saastr.com/what-17000-subscription-apps-tell-us-about-free-trial-length-annual-plans-convert-86-better-with-30-day-trials-monthly-tops-out-at-two-weeks-and-ai-apps-hit-a-wall-at-16-days). Interpretation: the decision is made in the first session, which argues for showing real value inside that session, not after a multi-step gate.
- Trial length: summary reports trials of 17-32 days have median conversion about 42.5% vs 25.5% for trials of 4 days or less (secondary, same SaaStr and Pricepush links). Cross-category and mostly annual plans. This is evidence that a 24 h trial is at the short, low-converting end of what the industry runs; it is NOT evidence that dating trials convert at that rate. [unverified for dating / mobile-money markets]
- Early cancellation: 30% of annual subscribers cancel within the first month (RevenueCat 2025 highlight, secondary: https://subscriptioninsider.com/article-type/news/revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps-2025-report-ais-dominance-retention-challenges-and-the-shift-away-from-pure-subscriptions).
- Not found: Adjust / AppsFlyer published trial or paywall conversion figures; I did not retrieve any. [unverified] Do not cite them.

### 1.2 Dating-app freemium practice

All secondary, consumer-press level; limits change often and by market. Re-check in-app before relying on any.

- Hinge: free tier reportedly 8 likes/day, see who liked you one at a time, unlimited messaging with matches; paid tiers add unlimited likes, see all likes at once, advanced filters (https://www.thestar.co.uk/business/consumer/best-dating-apps-free-uk-valentines-day-2025-tinder-hinge-4986206 , secondary).
- Tinder: free swipe cap, paid "Gold" adds unlimited likes, see who likes you, monthly boost, rewinds (same source). Bumble: paid tier adds unlimited likes and "Beeline" (see who already liked you) (same source).
- Pattern: across the big apps, free users can SEE profiles and MESSAGE after a match; what is sold is volume (likes), visibility (boost), and the "who liked you" reveal. Free messaging after match is the norm at Hinge (swipestats.io summary: https://www.swipestats.io/blog/free-messaging-dating-sites , secondary). None of the above gate seeing profiles behind payment or ID. Whether any of them require ID before browsing: Tinder's Face Check is required for new users in California and seven countries before use (see verification doc), so a verify-first model exists at scale, but it is a quick liveness selfie, not a government ID plus document capture, and the result is that users still get the app. See verification-and-trust.md.
- Self-reported: Tinder said ID-verified users saw a 67% increase in matches (https://www.globaldatinginsights.com/news/tinder-trials-new-photo-id-verification/ , company claim, not independent).

### 1.3 Regulators and dark patterns in dating subscriptions

- FTC v. Match Group: settlement announced 2025-08-12, $14 million redress, permanent injunction against misrepresenting guarantees; allegations concerned the "free six-month" guarantee (few people actually got it; users were billed another term), a cancellation flow an internal deck allegedly called "hard to find, tedious, and confusing", and terminating accounts of users who lost billing disputes (secondary: https://www.insideprivacy.com/consumer-protection/ftc-secures-14-million-settlement-with-match-group-over-deceptive-subscription-practices/ , https://tcpaworld.com/2025/08/12/ftc-says-its-over-dating-service-operator-match-group-to-pay-14m-for-deceptive-ads-and-cancellation-policies , https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/advertising-marketing-branding/1666962/match-group-to-pay-%2414m-in-ftc-settlement-over-alleged-deceptive-and-unfair-marketing-and-online-subscription-practices). The FTC primary press release was not reachable. Allegations are the FTC's; Match's position is not reported in these sources.
- UK CMA online dating services case closed 2018 with undertakings including clear warnings before auto-renewal of long subscriptions and not misleading about member numbers (secondary summary of https://www.gov.uk/cma-cases/online-dating-services ; page not directly fetched). UK DMCC Act subscription regime (pre-contract info, reminders, 14-day cooling-off windows incl. one after a free trial ends): not yet in force; one law-firm note says spring 2027 at the earliest (https://www.hsfkramer.com/notes/crt/2026-04/time-to-start-preparing-for-the-new-uk-subscription-contracts-regime ; also https://connectontech.bakermckenzie.com/dmcc-consumer-protection-spotlight-on-consumer-subscriptions/). Not applicable to Uganda; useful as a direction-of-travel benchmark.
- Norwegian Consumer Council "Out of Control" (Jan 2020): concerned data sharing by Grindr, Tinder and others with ad-tech, not subscription design. 135 third-party services in 10 Android apps tested; formal complaints against Grindr and ad-tech firms (https://www.thelocal.com/20200114/norway-consumer-rights-group-accuses-tinder-grindr-of-illegally-sharing-data). Relevance to us: reinforces CLAUDE.md rules 3, 4, 10 (no third-party SDK data leakage), not paywall design. I did not find a Norwegian Council dating-subscription dark-patterns action. [unverified]
- ASA (UK) rulings on dating-app trials: none found. [unverified]
- Apple: auto-renewable subscriptions must "provide ongoing value", last at least seven days; free trials permitted; subscribers must not have to "post on social media, upload contacts, check in a certain number of times" to get what they paid for (guideline 3.1.2(a), https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/).
- Google Play (Payments policy, subscriptions section; fetched only via search summary): offers must state trial duration, that the user will be auto-enrolled in a paid plan at the end, and how to cancel; violations include emphasising "free" without making the automatic charge clear (https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9900533). Apps selling subscriptions must disclose how to manage/cancel. Primary page not fetched; re-verify wording.
- Mapping to our rules: our trial has no payment details and no auto-conversion, which sidesteps the largest source of regulator cases (surprise charge after trial). Store-billed subscriptions still auto-renew by store design; disclosure must appear on the purchase screen in-app, not only in store metadata.

### 1.4 Emerging markets, mobile money, Uganda

- Mobile money is the payment rail: regulator data for June 2023 reports 42.9 million registered and 26.4 million active mobile-money accounts (activity = a billable transaction in three months) (https://www.theeastafrican.co.ke/tea/business-tech/more-ugandans-turn-to-mobile-money-1442858 and https://itweb.africa/article/ugandas-digital-finance-growth-better-than-africas-average-says-regulator/xnklOvzLoNaM4Ymz , secondary news). FinScope 2018: 56% accessed mobile money vs 11% bank accounts (same). Uganda smartphone adoption 16% in 2021 per GSMA via UCC (secondary, old: same itweb article). Note the target segment (urban, verified, smartphone, marriage-seeking) is much narrower than these national figures.
- Prepaid, short-validity purchase is the local norm for digital services: MultiChoice sells 7-day DStv/GOtv packs from UGX 5,000 via mobile money (https://blueprint.ng/multichoice-announces-new-subscription-packages-for-dstv-gotv/ , secondary); MTN data bundles are sold daily/weekly/monthly. This supports our existing choice of non-auto-renewing prepaid web periods, and suggests offering short/small first purchases. No published data on app-subscription auto-renew rates or willingness to pay in Uganda found. [unverified] No ARPU figure for Uganda dating apps found. [unverified] Do not use any ARPU in business cases without our own test.
- Google Play carrier/mobile-money billing availability in Uganda: not established by this research. [unverified]; check Play Console payment-method availability for UG before committing to store-only billing.

## 2. What the evidence does and does not say

- Strong (primary): store rules require disclosed terms and no bait-and-switch; Apple discourages forced login/personal data before core value; Apple/Google reviewers need a demo path.
- Moderate (secondary, cross-category): hard paywalls convert more per download than freemium (~5x median) but with higher refunds; short trials convert worse than long; the decision happens in session one.
- Weak/absent: anything about dating specifically in Uganda; trial conversion for a 24 h no-card trial; effect of mandatory ID before browse on activation in Africa.
- The biggest unknown for our design is activation: what share of installs pass OTP + consent + ID + profile (>= 2 photos, description, core questionnaire) and reach a trial start. That is unmeasured; we should instrument funnel steps (no PII) before choosing, per rule 10.

## 3. Options for our model

| Option | What the user gets free | Gate | Pros | Cons / risks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Current: verify, go-live, 24 h trial, then paid browse + send | Nothing visible until trial | ID + trial | Strong safety and paywall simplicity; no free-rider load; clean rule 1 | User sees no value before heavy friction; 24 h is short for a marriage-intent user who checks in intermittently; browse blocked on expiry so churned users cannot even look; owner's complaint; likely poor activation [unverified magnitude] |
| B. Free browse tier + daily limits, paid for more (Hinge/Tinder pattern) | Browse N profiles/day, receive/accept interest, read | Verification only to send/be visible | Value first; supply visible; proven by market leaders; fits "who liked you" teaser | Needs limit design; freemium converts ~5x lower on median (secondary); fake-scarcity temptation conflicts with rule 9 if limits are manufactured; abuse by scraping needs backend rate limits |
| C. Timed trial of full features, started after seeing a preview | Browse preview (limited, no contact) then 24 h to 7 d trial | Trial start explicit | Keeps current trial plumbing; trial begins when user is engaged; longer trial converts better (secondary) | Still a clock; trial length vs abuse; one-trial-per-identity already exists via identity_hmac |
| D. Pay-to-message / pay-to-send-interest (browse free, receive free, sending is the paid act) | Browse, see compatibility, receive and accept interest | Payment to send interest or message | Aligns price with value moment (contact); mobile-money friendly as small prepaid packs; natural for marriage intent; receivers never pay | Women/men asymmetry risk; pay-per-action can feel transactional; needs clear pricing; lower revenue from window-shoppers |
| E. Pay-for-contact (pay to reveal phone/WhatsApp) | Everything until contact exchange | Payment to unlock contact | Maps to real-world value | Directly conflicts with our in-app chat model, safety (off-platform moves), and Phase 5 intent; high abuse and scam risk; not recommended |

## 4. Recommendation (separate from evidence; owner decision)

1. Move the paywall to the contact act, not the viewing act: let verified-or-previewing users browse a limited, honest feed; require trial/membership only to send interest and message (option B+D hybrid with a C-style trial). This is consistent with Hinge/Tinder/Bumble and with Apple's "describe value before asking" rule.
2. Keep the explicit, no-card trial (it is regulator-safe), but start it only when the user chooses to send their first interest, and consider longer than 24 h (test 3-7 days) because cross-category data show longer trials convert better (secondary) and marriage-seekers do not decide in a day. Flag as PENDING OD, behind a config value.
3. Limits must be real and server-enforced (rule 1) and disclosed truthfully; no fake scarcity or countdowns (rule 9).
4. Prepaid mobile-money periods stay non-renewing; consider a small low-price first period (price points need local testing). Prices: [unverified, test].
5. Always keep read-only chats, received interests and account controls for expired users (already in 10.7); add "browse limited feed" for expired users so churn does not equal blindness.
6. Measure before committing: funnel instrumentation (no PII) for each step; A/B only if volume allows, otherwise ship B+D and watch activation and trial-start rates.
7. Build a store-review demo path (guideline 2.1(a)) regardless.
